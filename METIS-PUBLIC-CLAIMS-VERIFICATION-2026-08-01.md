# Metis Cortex — Public Privacy & Security Claims Verification

**Prepared:** 1 August 2026
**Scope:** every specific privacy/security claim on the public site (Home.tsx, the Safety page, the
Privacy Policy, Terms of Use, and the public chat assistant's own system prompt). General marketing
language ("premium," "organiser, not a lawyer") is out of scope — this covers only claims that assert
a specific technical or data-handling fact a reader might rely on.

**Method:** each claim checked against the real code and real production configuration — not against
what was intended or previously written. One real correction came out of this pass; it's already fixed
and deployed (see #3 below).

---

## Classification key

- ✅ **Verified** — checked directly against real code/config/production state, or against the
  third-party's own published terms.
- 🟡 **Partially verified** — the underlying mechanism is real and checked, but some detail (an exact
  retention period, a specific certification) isn't independently confirmed.
- 🔴 **Unsupported** — asserted without a real basis. None currently on the site as of this pass (see
  the one correction below).

---

## Claims checked

**1. "Every matter is isolated to your account... nobody else's matter can reach yours"**
(Home.tsx, Privacy, Safety, public chat prompt)
✅ **Verified.** Checked the actual storage-proxy authorization code
(`server/_core/storageProxy.ts`) — every document read resolves the record the key belongs to and
checks it against the requesting user's ID, returning 404 (not the file) for anything unowned.
Database-level tenancy checks (`ctx.user.id` scoping) have been adversarially tested multiple times
during development, including a real cross-tenant leak that was found, confirmed, and fixed (see the
incident-response plan §7).

**2. "A query for another user's matter or documents is rejected at the database level, not just
hidden in the interface"** (Safety, Privacy)
✅ **Verified.** Same basis as #1 — confirmed in the actual authorization code, not just asserted.

**3. "Documents you upload are stored in Cloudflare R2 object storage, encrypted at rest"**
(Privacy Policy, as originally published this morning)
🔴 **Was unsupported — now corrected, twice.** First pass: checked `server/storage.ts`'s
driver-selection logic against real production config — `fly.toml` hardcodes `STORAGE_DRIVER=local`,
no R2 credentials exist in Fly's secrets, no R2 bucket for this project exists at all (confirmed via
`wrangler r2 bucket list`). Documents are on a Fly.io volume. Corrected the policy to say so —
**but the corrected wording itself then claimed the volume was simply "not backed up," which was also
wrong.** Ran `flyctl volumes snapshots list` against the real production volume (not just trusted
`fly.toml`'s own comment): Fly's automatic scheduled snapshots are enabled, 3 real snapshots existed,
5-day retention. The policy now states precisely what's verified — hardware-local, not replicated,
Fly's own snapshots exist but aren't an independent, restore-tested backup — rather than either the
original wrong claim or an overcorrected wrong one. See the subprocessor register §1 for full detail
and the exact commands run.

**4. "Documents are encrypted in transit and at rest"** (Home.tsx, Safety, Privacy, public chat)
🟡 **Was imprecise — now corrected.** The original phrasing bundled the upload connection (browser to
server, genuinely transit, genuinely TLS-encrypted) with the server-to-volume write (local filesystem
I/O, not "transit" in any meaningful security sense, and not separately encrypted by the application —
confirmed no app-level encryption exists in `server/storage.ts`, `grep`'d directly). Every occurrence
across Home.tsx, the Safety page, the Privacy Policy, and the public chat assistant's own system
prompt now states the two facts separately and precisely: the connection is encrypted in transit
(HTTPS/TLS, confirmed via `force_https = true` in `fly.toml`), and the volume is encrypted at rest by
Fly at the disk level (confirmed via `flyctl volumes list` — `ENCRYPTED: true`) — provider-managed,
not something Metis does itself.

**5. "We don't use your documents or your matter to train any AI model" / "nothing is used to train
any AI model"** (Home.tsx, Safety, Terms, public chat)
✅ **Verified** against Anthropic's own published commercial API terms — data submitted to or
generated by the API is not used to train their models under standard commercial terms. This is the
one external claim in the corpus that rests on a third party's word rather than something checkable in
our own code, which is disclosed as such in the subprocessor register.

**6. "We don't run advertising trackers or sell any information to third parties"** (Privacy)
✅ **Verified.** Searched the entire client codebase for any analytics/tracking script (Google
Analytics, Meta Pixel, Hotjar, Segment, Mixpanel, PostHog, or any generic `gtag`/`analytics` call) —
zero matches. There is no tracking of any kind on the site currently, not just a policy against using
it.

**7. "We haven't yet had an independent security audit or formal compliance certification"**
(Safety, Privacy)
✅ **Verified as an accurate absence claim** — there is no certification or audit report to point to,
and none is claimed elsewhere on the site. This is the correct, honest framing and needed no change.

**8. "Aligned with the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and the Australian Privacy Principles"** (footer)
🟡 **Partially verified.** The product's actual practices (collect only what's needed, tell users what's
collected and why, provide a way to access/correct/delete) are consistent with APP principles in
substance — but "aligned with" is deliberately not "certified compliant with," and no formal APP
compliance review has been done. The wording already reflects this distinction correctly; flagging it
here as reviewed rather than changing it.

**9. Solicitor-side "DRAFT — wording not lawyer-reviewed. Do not send to a client."** banners on
generated costs documents (SessionReview.tsx)
✅ **Verified** — checked the actual component code; the banner renders unconditionally whenever a
generated document exists, not just in some states.

**10. "Metis never edits or deletes a document you've uploaded"** (Safety)
✅ **Verified** — no delete/edit code path exists in the chat/matter-assistant flow; document
deletion (where it exists at all) is a separate, user-initiated action outside the AI's control path.

---

## Outcome

Of 10 specific claims checked, 2 needed correction (document storage location, and the precision of
the transit/at-rest encryption claim — both on the same underlying topic), 1 is correctly hedged
already and needed no change, and 7 hold up against direct verification against real code, real
production config, or a third party's own published terms.

**A second-order lesson from this pass, worth stating plainly:** the first storage correction was
itself re-checked and found to contain a new, different overcorrection ("not backed up," when Fly's
own snapshots do exist). A claims-verification pass has to apply the same scrutiny to its own
corrections as to the original claims — fixing a wrong claim isn't finished until the fix itself has
been verified against real evidence, not just asserted with more confidence than the error it
replaced.

**What this pass does not cover** — flagged so it doesn't read as broader than it is: the accuracy of
subprocessors' own published claims about themselves (Anthropic's, Resend's, and Fly's security
posture is taken from their own public documentation, not independently audited by us), and anything
not yet built (the solicitor-side recording-consent workflow doesn't exist yet, so there's no claim
about it to verify).
