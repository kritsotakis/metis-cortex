## The founder frame (use as the opener, every conversation)

The Mom-Test discipline is "don't pitch." The founder story isn't a pitch — it's the credential. Lead every conversation with a version of this:

> *"I'm not selling anything today. I've been the client in three rounds of AU family-law process — my own divorce, the child and family matters that followed, and a current FCFCOA application for a child-support stay order that I'm preparing self-represented because I can't afford a solicitor. The documentation burden on the client side made each round 5x harder than it needed to be — and my read is that the solicitor side has a parallel problem that nobody's solved either. I'm researching that with 10 NSW family-law practitioners before building any more of this. Could I ask you about your last week?"*

**What this does:**
- Establishes lived-experience credibility in 30 seconds (irreplaceable, no competitor has it)
- Distinguishes from sales-call openers (they'll relax)
- Names BOTH product sides (solicitor + client) so the "Metis-prepared client" probe in section 5d lands naturally later
- Anchors the rest of the conversation on past behaviour, per Mom-Test rule #2

**What it doesn't do:**
- Doesn't ask for sympathy
- Doesn't pitch a product
- Doesn't claim expertise in legal practice (only in being the client)

## The single most important thing to learn (sharpened from v1)

v1 asked: *will a family lawyer record a client conference?*
v2 asks: **will a family lawyer record a client conference, AND if so, is the AustLII-live + family-law-current-law cognition + consent-workflow depth worth more to them than Law Brief AI's existing traction?**
**v2.1 (patch 2026-05-30 pm) adds:** AND — would they refer unprepared prospects to a tool that helps the client gather + organise documents before the first consult, or would they see that as a threat to their first-30-min framing control?

If they won't record, the solicitor-side product doesn't work — but if they would record AND Law Brief AI's "we have customers, you don't" is enough to close the conversation, the wedge isn't defensible either. AND if they HATE the idea of a Metis-prepared client, the client-side product breaks the solicitor channel — that changes Year-2 strategy. Probe all three.

## Two cohorts, two gates (Gemini-vs-Manus synthesis)

| Cohort | Who | Why | Send order |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Accredited Specialists** | The 14 Law Society NSW Accredited Specialists (Family Law) in the staged outreach list | Credibility ladder. They have paralegals + entrenched workflows → they won't be first paying customers, BUT their feedback is the highest-signal on whether the workflow stands up. One signed endorsement = transformative reach into the rest of the market. | Send first (week 1). Target: 5-7 conversations. |
| **B — Overwhelmed suburban generalists** | Solo practitioners doing ~30-50% family law, no support staff, in the 9 "Verify" + 5 "No" rows of the staged list, plus a second sweep of LinkedIn / Law Society directory for NW Sydney / Sutherland / Northern Beaches "general practice incl family law" descriptions | Highest pain, fastest yes. ## The interview script v2 (≈15 min, conversational)

The v1 sections 1-4c are intact. **Replace section 3 (Recording — the crux)** with the v2 version below. **Add new sections 5b and 5c.**

### 3 v2 — Recording (reframed past-behaviour + matter-type-specific)

Old (v1): *"How would you feel about a recording of the conference sitting on the file — does that protect you, or expose you?"*

The problem with the v1 framing: it's a hypothetical feeling-question. Mom-Test rule #2 says ask about specifics that already happened.

**Use this instead:**

- *"When's the last time you had a client conference where you wished you had it on the record? What was happening?"* (past behaviour, lets them surface the pain unprompted)
- *"And the opposite — when's the last time you were grateful nothing was on the record? Walk me through that."* (the discoverability fear from ChatGPT — listen for "if there'd been a recording, I'd be in trouble")
- *"If I gave you a tool that handled the consent piece for you — explicit, timestamped, separately stored — would the recording sit differently for you? Why / why not?"* (this is the only feeling-question we keep, because it tests the consent reframe directly)
- *"What about by matter type — does a property settlement feel different to a coercive-control matter? Tell me about both."* (NEW — Manus/Gemini insight on per-matter-type mode preference)

**What you're listening for:**
- 🟢 They name a specific recent conference where a recording would have helped (paying signal)
- 🟢 They name conditions that would make recording acceptable, unprompted ("if the client agreed up front and I could turn it off mid-meeting if needed")
- 🟢 They distinguish between matter types (property = ok, FV = no) → confirms both-modes architecture
- 🔴 "Recording would never work in family law" with no specifics → vertical may be wrong; probe whether post-event lawyer-only dictation would work instead
- 🔴 Polite "I could see that being useful" → fake signal

### 5b — The competitor probe (NEW, mandatory)

The seam is contested. The interview has to surface whether they know about Law Brief AI / AI Legal Assistant, and if so, what their read is.

**Ask after section 5 (Current tools) and BEFORE section 6 (Money):**

- *"Are there any AI tools for legal practice you've actively looked at or trialled in the last 6 months? Which ones, and what made you stop or keep going?"* (open-ended; surface their own awareness without naming names)
- *If they don't name competitors:* don't volunteer them. Note "competitor-aware: no" in the capture sheet.
- *If they name Law Brief AI / AI Legal Assistant / Smokeball Archie / LEAP Matter AI:* dig.
  - *"What did they show you?"*
  - *"Did you talk to anyone who'd actually used it in practice — what did they say?"*
  - *"What stopped you from going further, or what made you stay?"*
- *If they DO want a direct comparison and ask:* use the honest framing below.

### 5c — The "vs Law Brief AI" honest framing (when they ask)

*"Law Brief AI's been in market longer and their pitch is broader — they capture phone calls + in-person conferences, draft a file note + a client letter, and they have a Sunshine Coast family-law firm using it now. What we built is narrower: live AustLII case-law search during the conference, family-law cognition tuned to the post-2024 parenting + post-June-2025 codified property reforms, and a consent + privilege workflow built for NSW specifically. Worth a 15-minute look to see if that depth is worth more to you than their head-start — and if it's not, that's a fine answer too."*

**Why this lands:**
- Acknowledges Law Brief AI's lead without conceding the market
- Names the four UNIQUE rows from the teardown without sounding like a deck
- Permits the "no" answer ("if it's not, that's a fine answer too") — Mom-Test discipline
- Doesn't promise what we don't have (no testimonials, no SOC 2)

### 5d — The "Metis-prepared client" probe (NEW v2.1, mandatory)

Ask after section 5c (the vs-Law-Brief-AI honest framing). Mom-Test discipline applies — ask about past behaviour, not hypotheticals about Metis.

**Past-behaviour entry:**
- *"Walk me through the last time a prospect arrived for a first consult genuinely well-prepared — documents organised, chronology already in their head, asset positions roughly mapped. What did that consult look like compared to the average?"*
- *"And the opposite — when's the last time you had a consult where the first 30 minutes was just getting their documents into a usable state? What happened?"*

**Then the strategic test:**
- *"If a prospect could arrive with a document inventory + chronology + draft asset/liability schedule + the matter pre-categorised against the relevant FCFCOA framework — produced by a tool, but reviewed by them — would that be a help or a threat? Walk me through your reaction."*
- *"Would you actively refer prospects to a tool like that BEFORE they book a consult with you? Why or why not?"*

**Then the architecture question — Amica framing helps here:**
- *"Amica (amica.gov.au) handles amicable separations — agreements, parenting plans, property division by consent. We're thinking about the matters Amica explicitly excludes: contested, FV, child-support / DPO, anything needing a court document instead of an agreement. Does that line up with the matters you actually see, or am I missing it?"*

**What you're listening for:**
- 🟢 They name a specific recent well-prepared prospect AND describe time saved
- 🟢 They distinguish helpful preparation ("documents in order") from harmful preparation ("client arrives with the wrong framing locked in")
- 🟢 They volunteer that Amica's exclusion list (contested + FV + child support) is exactly the matters they spend the most time on
- 🟢 They'd actively refer prospects to a Metis-Client-style tool BEFORE the first consult — flywheel signal
- 🔴 They see Metis-prepared clients as "an opinion competing with mine before I even meet them" — poison pill for the solicitor channel
- 🔴 Polite "that sounds useful" with no past-behaviour anchor

**The decision math from this probe:**

| Cohort A signal | Cohort B signal | Year-1 strategy |
|---|---|---|
| 🟢 Refer + see flywheel | 🟢 Same | Dual-track: build solicitor wedge AND client-side Phase 1 in parallel |
| 🟢 Refer + see flywheel | 🔴 Threat | Solicitor wedge first; client-side only via referral partnership (Phase 2) |
| 🔴 Threat | 🟢 Same | Skip Cohort A entirely; rebuild Cohort B around generalists who want client-prep |
| 🔴 Threat | 🔴 Same | Client-side product = Phase 3 minimum; solicitor wedge only for Year-1 |

### 7 — Per-matter-type mode preference (NEW, optional probe)

Only ask if the recording conversation in section 3 v2 surfaced any matter-type distinction. Otherwise skip.

- *"If we had two modes — live record during the conference, or a 5-minute lawyer-only dictation immediately after — which would you actually use for an initial consult? For an FDR-prep meeting? For a final-orders sign-off?"*

Maps the live-vs-post-event preference matrix per matter type. Output goes into the post-call action sheet.

## Pass threshold v2 (sharpened from v1)

The v1 thresholds stay:
- ≥ **7 of 10** describe consult→file-note (or "you never told me that") as top-3 weekly pain, unprompted
- ≥ **4 of 10** would record AND say they'd pay to fix it

**Add two v2 thresholds (both must hold):**
- ≥ **6 of 10** give a substantive answer to "what would have to be true for you to record" rather than "I wouldn't"
- ≥ **5 of 10** prefer the Metis Cortex depth-of-cognition story over Law Brief AI's traction story when given the honest 5c framing

**If any of the four thresholds miss → fold or pivot per the kill signals below.**

## Kill signals (sharpened — two new from synthesis)

The 60-day gate from STATUS (10 interviews → ONE paying invoice by day 45 → testimonial + 2 referrals by day 60) is sharpened with these additional fold-or-pivot triggers:

1. **Can't win "vs Law Brief AI" cleanly in 7+ of 10 conversations** → fold OR explore partnership/acquisition conversation with Law Brief AI directly
2. **Can't get a "yes I'd record under workflow X" answer in 6+ of 10** → pivot to post-event lawyer-only dictation as primary mode (Gemini's "Post-Consult Solo Scribe" pivot); live recording demoted to a feature
3. **Recording is culturally rejected in all 10** → vertical is wrong; fold or move to psychology vertical (AI Legal Assistant's territory but adjacent)
4. **Mom-Test cohort uniformly names SOC 2 / ISO as gating** → AI Legal Assistant has the market; partner or fold
