## Answer 1 — "What documents do I need for a parenting matter / custody dispute?"

> Been through this myself as a self-represented parent, so here's the practical list courts
> actually work from:
>
> - Each child's birth certificate (from your state's Births Deaths and Marriages — it's a
>   posted paper certificate, so order it early, not the week you file)
> - A real record of the actual care arrangements — a dated diary/calendar of who the kids
>   lived with and when, roughly the last 12 months. What actually happened carries more
>   weight than what either parent says was agreed.
> - Any existing orders, parenting plans or consent orders
> - School enrolment records, medical records
> - Messages/emails with the other parent about arrangements
>
> The one that catches most people out: the **section 60I certificate**. Before you can file
> for parenting orders, you generally need a certificate from an accredited Family Dispute
> Resolution practitioner showing mediation was attempted. No certificate (or filed exemption
> affidavit) = the registry rejects the filing outright. There are exemptions (urgency, family
> violence) but whether one applies to you is a question for a duty lawyer or Legal Aid
> (1300 888 529 in NSW), not a form.
>
> Also filed with every parenting application, which nobody warns you about: a Notice of child
> abuse, family violence or risk (mandatory even with no allegations), a Genuine Steps
> Certificate, and an Undertaking as to disclosure.
>
> Longer version with the why-each-item-matters: metiscortex.au/guides/parenting-matter-documents-checklist
> (disclosure: I built the site — the guides are free)

## Answer 2 — "I was fired unfairly, what do I do?"

> The single most important thing before anything else: **you have 21 days from the dismissal
> taking effect to lodge with the Fair Work Commission** (s394 Fair Work Act), and they only
> extend it in genuinely exceptional circumstances. Wanting legal advice first doesn't count.
> If you're anywhere near the deadline, lodge the Form F2 online at fwc.gov.au now — it doesn't
> need to be a finished case, it needs to be in on time. You build the substance afterwards.
>
> While the clock runs, gather: your termination letter (it fixes the exact date the 21 days
> run from), employment contract, recent payslips, and any warnings or performance reviews —
> if the employer claims performance, whether real warnings exist is usually where these
> cases turn.
>
> Whether YOUR dismissal was unfair is a judgement call — a community legal centre or an
> employment lawyer's free first consult is the right place for that.
>
> Longer version: metiscortex.au/guides/unfair-dismissal-21-day-deadline (disclosure: my site,
> guides are free)

## Answer 3 — "Landlord is keeping my bond, what can I do?" (NSW)

> Bond disputes at NCAT are decided on paper, not on who sounds more reasonable. What actually
> decides them:
>
> - **Entry and exit condition reports** — the first thing NCAT compares. Your agent must
>   provide copies if you don't have them.
> - **Dated photos/video** from move-in and move-out — usually the deciding evidence on
>   damage-vs-fair-wear-and-tear
> - The tenancy agreement and a clean rent ledger
> - If a termination notice was involved: check the notice period was right (90 days
>   no-grounds periodic, 30 days end of fixed term, 14 days breach — Residential Tenancies
>   Act 2010). Wrong notice period is often the whole case.
>
> Practical notes: the bond sits with NSW Fair Trading's Rental Bonds Online, not the
> landlord — their records settle any argument about the amount. And the Tenants' Union of
> NSW gives free advice before a hearing.
>
> Longer version: metiscortex.au/guides/ncat-bond-dispute-what-to-bring (disclosure: my site,
> guides are free)
