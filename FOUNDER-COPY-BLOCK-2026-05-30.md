**Date:** 2026-05-30
**Use:** the founder story is the single biggest credibility unlock metiscortex.au has. All 4 external reviewers flagged it as invisible on the live site. This doc has three versions of the same 3-act story, sized for different surfaces. **Read once; correct anything that's wrong; then I'll wire the medium version into the site and the short version into the Mom-Test outreach.**

**The 3-act spine (don't change):**
1. **Enterprise IT engineer** — 6 years at the bank-and-telco scale (MCP / MCSA / MCSE / Cisco / Citrix) before the restaurant. Gave him the systems-thinking lens.
2. **Restaurant operator** — 21 years running Limani Seafood, Narrabeen. Sold 2025. Gave him the operator-pain-in-the-bones lens.
3. **Family-law client, three rounds** — own divorce, then the child / family matters that followed, now a current FCFCOA s.111C stay order application prepared self-represented. Gave him the *user* lens on a system that doesn't have one.

The thing that's actually rare: act 1 (systems) + act 2 (operator) + act 3 (lived family-law user) combined in one founder. Most legaltech founders have one of these. None has all three.

## Version 1 — LONG (site About / Founder section — ~180 words)

> I'm Peter Kritsotakis. I started in enterprise IT — six years across banks and telcos as a systems engineer (MCSA, MCSE, Cisco, Citrix). Then I ran Limani Seafood in Narrabeen for 21 years and sold it in 2025. Two careers, both built on the same thing: keeping a system standing when twenty things are going wrong at once.
>
> Metis Cortex came from a third experience I didn't plan on. I've been the client in three rounds of AU family-law process — my own divorce, the child and family matters that followed, and a current FCFCOA child-support stay application I'm preparing self-represented because I couldn't justify the solicitor cost.
>
> Each round, the documentation burden on the client side made everything five times harder than it needed to be. Each round, I realised the solicitor side has a parallel problem nobody's solved — capturing what was actually said in the conference, finding the right authority during the meeting, drafting the file note and proposal before the client leaves the room.
>
> Metis is what I wish I'd had on both sides.

**Notes for Peter:**
- "couldn't justify the solicitor cost" — that's the framing if it's true; if it's "couldn't afford" or "chose self-rep for other reasons," correct it. The honest framing is the right one. Both work.
- The "sold it in 2025" is from memory — confirm the year/exit phrasing.
- The "20 things going wrong at once" line is the operator-credibility hook — could be sharper if you have a specific anecdote (Friday-night-service-with-a-broken-walk-in is the classic).
