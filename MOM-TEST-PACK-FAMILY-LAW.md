# Metis Cortex — NSW Family-Law Mom-Test Pack

**Goal:** find out — cheaply, before building more — whether NSW family-law sole/micro practitioners have a painful, expensive problem that Metis Cortex solves, and whether they'd *pay*. This is validation, not selling.

**The single most important thing to learn:** will a family lawyer **record a client conference**, and do they see a recording as protection or exposure? If they won't record, the product doesn't work — so probe this first.

---

## The 5 Mom-Test rules (read before every call)
1. Talk about **their life and past behaviour**, not your idea. Never pitch.
2. Ask about **specifics that already happened**, not hypotheticals ("tell me about the last time…", not "would you…").
3. **Compliments are worthless.** "That sounds great / useful / I'd definitely use that" = a fail signal, not a win.
4. **Commitment is the only real signal** — time, a referral, or money. Anything future-tense with no past behaviour behind it is noise.
5. **Talk less, listen more.** If you're talking, you're not learning.

---

## Who to interview
NSW **family-law sole practitioners and 2–4 lawyer firms** (the people who run their own conferences and write their own file notes — not BigLaw). Aim for **10 conversations**, people you don't already know.

**Where to find them:**
- Law Society of NSW "Find a Lawyer" directory → filter Family Law, by suburb.
- NSW family-law / sole-practitioner Facebook & LinkedIn groups.
- Local law associations; FCFCOA precinct networking.
- Warm intros via your own legal contacts (the divorce/DPO network).

## The interview script (≈15 min, conversational — don't read like a survey)

**Warm-up (frame as research, not a pitch)**
> "I'm trying to understand how small family-law practices actually run day to day — not selling anything. Can I ask you about your last week?"

**1. Workflow / the file-note pain (past behaviour)**
- "Walk me through the last new-client conference you ran — from them sitting down to the file note being written. When did you actually write it up, and how long did it take?"
- "When's the last time you were doing file notes or letters after hours? What was that like?"

**2. The "you never told me that" pain (your lived insight)**
- "Has a client ever come back later and said 'you never told me that' or 'I told you about X'? Tell me what happened — how did you handle it?"
- "How do you protect yourself today against a dispute about what was said in a conference?"

**3. Recording — the crux (mutual-protection probe, both directions)**
- "Do you ever record client conferences? Why / why not?"
- "How would you feel about a recording of the conference sitting on the file — does that protect you, or expose you?" *(let them talk — this is the make-or-break answer)*
- "If a **client asked you** to record the conference, would you? What would have to be true for you to be comfortable?"

**4. On-the-spot research (value + AI-accuracy trust)**
- "When an unexpected issue comes up mid-conference, what do you do — research later, wing it, book a follow-up?"
- "Have you tried any AI for legal research? What happened — did you trust it?" *(surfaces the hallucination fear directly)*

**4b. Costs disclosure + file note (the compliance pain — research-confirmed)**
- "Walk me through how you produce the **costs disclosure** for a new matter today — template, dictate, by hand? How long does it take, and has it ever caused you grief?" *(s174/s178 is the highest-risk, most painful artefact — listen for real pain)*
- "After a conference, what do you actually have to produce before the matter can move — file note, costs disclosure, retainer, client-care letter? Which is the most painful?"
- *(Do NOT ask "would you like AI to do this." Listen for whether producing these is a top pain. If a tool drafted the costs disclosure + a defensible file note from the conference, that's the sharpest hook — but only if they name it as a pain unprompted.)*

**4c. Family-law process gates (does the product need to flag these?)**
- "How do you keep on top of the process steps — s60I/FDR certificate, pre-action steps, financial-disclosure obligations, time limits? Ever been caught out by one?" *(tests whether AI gate-flagging is valued or noise)*

**5. Current tools (incumbent + would-they-add-beside)**
- "What practice-management software are you on (LEAP / Smokeball / other)? What made you pick it?"
- "When's the last time it frustrated you? What happened?"
- "Have you added any tools *alongside* it? What made you add (or not add) something new?"

**6. Money / decision (budget + who decides)**
- "What do you spend monthly on practice software, and who decides when you add something?"
- "Last time you paid for a new tool — what made it worth it?"

**Commitment ask (only if there's genuine heat):**
> "I'm building something in this area and I'd value your eyes on it for 15 minutes next week — could we book a time?"
*(A booked slot / an intro to a peer / "can I be a paid beta" = real. "Send me a link" with no time = polite no.)*

## Scoring — green light vs polite-but-fake yes

| 🟢 Real signal | 🔴 Fake / noise |
|---|---|
| Unprompted emotion about after-hours notes or a "you never told me that" dispute | "That sounds really useful" |
| Has **already tried/paid** for something to fix it | "AI is the future, good on you" |
| Names a dollar figure of wasted time, or a specific recent incident | Future-tense enthusiasm, no past behaviour |
| Open to recording **if X** — gives concrete conditions | Vague "maybe / depends" with no detail |
| Books the 15-min slot / offers a referral | "Send me a link" + no time committed |

**Pass threshold (per Crossing-the-Chasm beachhead test):**
- ≥ **7 of 10** describe consult→file-note (or the "you never told me that" risk) as a **top-3 weekly pain, unprompted**, AND
- ≥ **4 of 10** would record **and** say they'd pay to fix it.
- If recording gets a hard no from most → **stop** (or pivot to a non-recording capture method). That's the cheap, valuable answer.
