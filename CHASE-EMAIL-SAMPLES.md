# Chase Email Samples — Three Tones

**Show her these to anchor the tone conversation.** Pull up on your phone if she asks "what would the emails actually look like?"

The point is NOT to commit to wording. The point is to surface which tone band her firm operates in — then we draft real templates in her voice during the kickoff.

---

## Sample A — Formal / Professional

**For:** corporate clients, audit/SMSF clients, traditional firms

> **Subject:** Invoice #INV-1234 — payment outstanding (A$2,450)
>
> Dear [Client First Name],
>
> A friendly reminder that Invoice INV-1234, issued 15 March 2026 for A$2,450, is now 14 days overdue.
>
> Payment can be made via the link below, or please contact our office on (02) 9XXX XXXX if you'd like to discuss payment options.
>
> [Pay invoice now →]
>
> Kind regards,
> [Firm Name]
> [Partner Name] · Principal

---

## Sample B — Warm / Professional (RECOMMENDED DEFAULT for most AU SMB practices)

**For:** owner-managed small businesses, sole traders, long-term clients

> **Subject:** Quick reminder — invoice #INV-1234
>
> Hi [First Name],
>
> Just touching base on invoice INV-1234 (A$2,450, sent 15 March). It's been a couple of weeks and we haven't seen payment land yet.
>
> No problem if there's something we should know about — happy to chat through it. If it's just slipped through, here's the payment link:
>
> [Pay invoice →]
>
> Cheers,
> [First Name from firm]

## Sample C — Direct / Escalated (for T+30 onwards)

**For:** repeat slow payers, clients who haven't responded to two prior reminders

> **Subject:** Invoice #INV-1234 (A$2,450) — action required
>
> Hi [Client First Name],
>
> Invoice INV-1234 for A$2,450, issued 15 March 2026, is now 32 days overdue. We've sent two reminders without response.
>

>
> [Pay invoice now →] · or call (02) 9XXX XXXX
>
> Regards,
> [Firm Name]

## What to ask her after she's seen them

> *"Which of these three sounds most like how your firm talks to clients? Or is the real voice somewhere between them?"*

Listen for:
- "Definitely not A — too cold for our clients" → warm-professional firm
- "B is too casual for our brand" → formal firm
- "We'd never escalate to a partner that early" → loose AR culture
- "Honestly, C is closer to what we should be doing" → her real frustration is talked-about, not done

Then ask:
> *"Are there clients you'd be MORE direct with? Or MORE polite?"*

That tells you how to segment her client list in the real workflow.

---

## What this is NOT

These samples are NOT what we'd send live. They're conversation anchors.

The real workflow drafts templates from:
- Her existing chase emails (best signal — get 2-3 samples during kickoff)
- Her firm's brand voice (formal vs warm)
- The specific invoice age bracket (T+7 ≠ T+45)
- The specific client segment (VIP ≠ standard ≠ repeat-slow-payer)

By day 7 of the pilot, every email goes out drafted in her voice, approved by her, sent on her schedule.
