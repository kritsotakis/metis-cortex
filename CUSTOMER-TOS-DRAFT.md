## 2. The Service

### 2.1 What we provide

Metis Cortex provides an AI voice receptionist service marketed under the name **"Zoe"** (the **"Service"**). The Service comprises:

(a) a **14-day install** during which we configure and deploy Zoe on a phone number you nominate (your existing number, ported in, or a new Twilio number we provision on your behalf);

(b) **ongoing operation** of Zoe to answer inbound calls, greet your callers, capture lead information, book appointments where configured, and route transcripts and notifications to your nominated CRM or inbox; and

(c) **standard support** as described in clause 9.

### 2.2 Underlying technology

The Service is built on third-party software-as-a-service platforms hosted in the United States, including without limitation:

- **Retell AI** (voice agent runtime and large language model orchestration);
- **GoHighLevel ("GHL")** (workflow, CRM, notifications); and
- **Twilio** (telephony, number provisioning and porting, call routing).

You acknowledge that the Service is dependent on these third-party platforms (the **"Upstream Providers"**) and that their availability, performance, terms and policies are outside our control. We will use reasonable efforts to monitor Upstream Provider status and escalate where incidents affect you.

### 2.3 Cross-border data transfer (Australian Privacy Principle 8.1)

You acknowledge and agree that, in the ordinary course of providing the Service, **call audio, transcripts, caller phone numbers, and any personal information disclosed by your callers** will be transmitted to and processed on servers located in the **United States** by the Upstream Providers. You authorise this disclosure and consent to it being made on your behalf, and you warrant that you will obtain any further consents required from your callers (see clause 5).

## 6. The Two Guarantees

We offer two stacked, independent guarantees. Both are **goodwill commercial guarantees** offered in addition to, and not in substitution for, your statutory rights under the Australian Consumer Law.

### 6.1 Operational Guarantee — "100% of missed calls handled + 10 hours saved in 30 days"

(a) **The promise.** In the **first 30 calendar days** after Zoe goes live (the **"Measurement Window"**), we promise that:

   (i) Zoe will **answer 100% of inbound calls** routed to it that connect successfully through the Twilio carrier (i.e. Zoe will not silently fail to pick up calls that the carrier has delivered to it); and

   (ii) Zoe will save you at least **10 hours of phone-handling time** compared with your pre-install baseline, calculated as the total connected-call duration handled by Zoe during the Measurement Window.

(b) **Measurement.**

   (i) "Calls answered" is measured by the Twilio call log: connected inbound calls to your Zoe-routed number where Zoe responded to the caller. "Missed" excludes calls where the caller hung up before connection, calls blocked by Twilio carrier-level fraud filters, and calls that failed to deliver due to Upstream Provider outages outside our control.

   (ii) "Hours saved" is the sum of connected-call durations handled by Zoe in the Measurement Window, drawn from the Twilio + Retell logs. We will provide a written report at the end of the Measurement Window showing the calculation.

(c) **The remedy.** If we materially fail either limb of the Operational Guarantee for reasons within our control (excluding Upstream Provider outages, Customer-caused delays, or breach by you of clause 5), we will refund the **A$1,500 setup fee** in full and you may terminate the agreement on 7 days' notice without further fees beyond accrued monthly charges to the date of notice.

(d) **Claim window.** A claim under this clause must be made in writing within 14 days of the end of the Measurement Window. Claims made later are not eligible.

### 6.2 Install Guarantee — "Live in 14 days or setup fee waived"

(a) **The promise.** We promise to make Zoe **"live"** within **14 calendar days** of the later of (i) signature of the Statement of Work, (ii) receipt of the cleared setup fee, and (iii) receipt of all Configuration Inputs reasonably requested by us.

(b) **"Live" means** Zoe is answering inbound test calls on the nominated phone number, completing a full intake flow end-to-end including notification delivery to your nominated CRM or inbox, and approved by you (acting reasonably) in writing.

(c) **The remedy.** If we fail to make Zoe live within 14 days for reasons within our control, we will **waive the A$1,500 setup fee** (refunded if already paid) and continue the install at no further setup cost. Monthly fees do not begin until Zoe is live.

(d) **Customer-caused delay.** Delays caused by you (including delay in providing Configuration Inputs, delay in approvals, late provision of access to your phone system or CRM, or porting delays caused by your existing carrier) extend the 14-day clock day-for-day.

### 6.3 No double-counting

The two guarantees are independent in scope but cap our refund liability under both at **A$1,500 (the setup fee)** in the aggregate.

# Lawyer Review Checklist

To assist counsel: please answer or comment on each of the following.

1. **Chain-of-consent enforceability (clause 4.2).** Is the Customer's contractual obligation to maintain caller-side disclosures sufficient to discharge our risk under the **NSW Surveillance Devices Act 2007** and the equivalent Vic, Qld, SA, WA, Tas, ACT and NT statutes — particularly in jurisdictions that may require all-party consent? Should clause 4.2 be strengthened (e.g. require specific website privacy-page wording, or a written attestation at install)?

2. **APP 8.1 cross-border consent (clause 4.4).** Is the Customer's authorisation in clause 4.4 sufficient to satisfy APP 8.1, given that the actual data subjects (the callers) are not parties to this contract? Do we need a model "caller-facing" disclosure paragraph that we mandate the Customer publish on their website / IVR?

3. **Privacy Act roles (clause 4.3).** Is the "Customer is APP entity / Metis Cortex is service provider" framing accurate, or are we more correctly characterised as a joint controller given the level of configuration and routing we control? Does this need a separate **Data Processing Addendum** rather than living inline?

4. **Australian Consumer Law and the limitation of liability cap (clause 7.2).** Is a "12 months of fees paid" cap defensible against a small-business customer protected by the unfair contract terms regime in **Schedule 2, Part 2-3** of the ACL (as amended by the Treasury Laws Amendment (More Competition, Better Prices) Act 2022)? Should we add a higher floor for breaches of clauses 4 and 8?

5. **Unfair Contract Terms regime.** Many of our customers will be small-business customers (under 100 employees / under A$10m turnover), so the UCT regime applies. Please flag any clause likely to be deemed "unfair" — particularly the unilateral variation clause (14), the indemnity (13), the termination-for-convenience clause (3.5 final paragraph), and the founding-cohort price-rise mechanism (3.1(c)).

6. **The two guarantees as "consumer guarantees" vs. "commercial guarantees".** Clauses 6.1 and 6.2 are framed as goodwill commercial guarantees. Do we need to also include the prescribed consumer-guarantee wording under the **Australian Consumer Law (s 102)** and the warranties-against-defects regime in regulation 90 of the Competition and Consumer Regulations 2010? Is the "claim window" of 14 days (clause 6.1(d)) enforceable, or could it be struck as unfair?

7. **Notifiable Data Breach timeline (clause 4.5).** Is the 72-hour notification window to the Customer reasonable, given that the Privacy Act itself only requires "as soon as practicable" notification by the APP entity to the OAIC? Should we instead align with "without undue delay"?

8. **Outbound calling carve-out (clauses 2.1 and 8(c)).** When we eventually launch outbound, what additional regulatory obligations attach (Spam Act, DNCR, ACMA telemarketing standards)? Should we draft an "Outbound Module Addendum" now so we are not rewriting the master Terms when we light it up?

9. **Pass-through Twilio costs (clause 3.2(e)).** The 10% margin on Twilio pass-through — is the disclosure adequate for ACL purposes, or do we need a more prominent "estimated monthly cost" notice? Any issue with our notify-above-A$100 threshold?

10. **Indemnity scope (clause 13).** The indemnity is broadly drafted in our favour. Given the UCT regime and the small-business profile, would it be safer to: (a) cap the indemnity at the 12-month-fees figure, (b) make it mutual, or (c) narrow it to clauses 4 and 8 only?

11. **Trust/trustee identification (clause 1).** We have identified the Provider as "Kritsotakis Investments Pty Ltd as trustee for the Kritsotakis Family Trust trading as Metis Cortex." Is the trustee-capacity wording correct, and should we add the trustee's right of indemnity from trust assets, or any "limited recourse" wording common in trust contracts? Is our address (1 Oxford Cl, Belrose) acceptable as a registered address for service?

12. **Force majeure including Upstream Provider outages (clause 10).** Treating "Upstream Provider outage / deprecation / termination" as a force-majeure event materially shifts risk to the Customer. Is this defensible under the UCT regime? Would a more measured carve-out (e.g. "force majeure for outages of less than 14 days, mutual termination for longer") be safer?
