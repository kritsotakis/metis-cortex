# Lawyer pack — what to send, and what to ask

Assembled 28 July 2026. Companion to `../LAWYER-BRIEF-2026-07-28.md`.

## The ask, in one line

*Which parts of this can be offered to other people, and on what conditions?*

Not a general risk review. One question, answerable in about half an hour.

---

## Send these four things

**1. `../LAWYER-BRIEF-2026-07-28.md`** — the brief itself. Read this first; the
rest is evidence for it.

**2. `CLIENT-AGREEMENT.md`** *(in this folder)* — what every user must accept
before they can open a matter. §5A of the brief asks whether it is doing what we
think it is. Worth their specific attention given *Van der Feltz*.

**3. One draft from the builder's own matter** — pick
`~/Desktop/dpo/drafts/02_affidavit.md`. A 27-paragraph affidavit with an
annexure schedule, produced from a folder of source documents.

This single file is the whole question made concrete. If "producing a document
from a body of facts, having analysed its legal effect" is legal practice, this
is the document that crosses the line — and it is the one capability that would
most help the people the tool is aimed at.

**4. `~/Desktop/dpo/CRITIQUE-DPO-2026-07-14.md`** — the adversarial critique
that found five material problems in the builder's own drafts. Arguably the most
valuable output and the hardest to categorise: it is information about how the
other side will argue, not advice about what to do.

## Do NOT send the whole matter

There are 14 drafts and 89 documents. Sending all of it invites an open-ended
review, which is the opposite of what the brief asks for, and it is billed by
the hour. Two examples make the point better than twenty.

They contain bankruptcy status, trust interests, financial position and a
medical report — send them over something appropriate, and say plainly that
they're the builder's own documents in his own matter.

## Offer a live walkthrough

Ten minutes on **metis-cortex.fly.dev** answers more than the brief does. Show:
the checklist and where it refers out to a lawyer; "Ask Metis" refusing to give
advice or cite a case; the exported pack and its "prepared by the client, not by
a lawyer" heading.

## The four answers worth leaving with

1. **Where is the line?** Checklist and explanation on one side, affidavit
   drafting on the other — is that right? (Brief §2)
2. **Does solicitor sign-off move it?** If a solicitor reviews and adopts the
   work, are they the one practising — and what would make that review real
   rather than a rubber stamp? (§3)
3. **Browser automation** — is a user-supervised co-pilot on myGov meaningfully
   different from a credential-holding bot? (§4A)
4. **Will you write the s174 wording?** The costs-disclosure generator carries a
   deliberate `[LAWYER TO CONFIRM EXACT WORDING]` placeholder. Given s178, we
   would rather they drafted it than checked ours. (§5B)

## What is already decided and not up for discussion

Stated so their time isn't spent on it:

- No case citations, ever — the tool refuses even correct ones
- Every output carries a not-a-lawyer heading
- The tool is invitation-only, free, and not open to the public
- Anything a solicitor says must not ship, will not ship
