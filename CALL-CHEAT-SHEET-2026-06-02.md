## OPENING — first 2 minutes

**Same opener every call. Anchor on credentials, not product.**

> *"Thanks for taking the time. I'll keep this to 15 minutes. I'm not here to pitch you anything — I'm doing research with NSW family-law specialists about how the conference-to-file-note workflow actually runs in practice, and what would have to be true for you to even consider AI in your practice. I'm an operator, not a lawyer — I've been on the client side of three family-law matters myself, which is what got me here. Can I ask you about your last week first?"*

**Why this opens cleanly:**
- "Not pitching" lowers their guard
- "Research with NSW family-law specialists" frames them as the expert (which they are)
- "Operator, not a lawyer" gets ahead of "are you one of us?"
- "Three rounds as the client" earns the 15 minutes
- "Your last week first" puts them in concrete past-behaviour, not abstract future hypothetical (Mom-Test rule #2)

## CORE QUESTIONS — in order, conversationally

### Workflow (the documentation tail)

1. **"Walk me through the last new-client conference you ran. From them sitting down to the file note being finished — when did you actually write it, and how long did it take?"**
2. **"When's the last time you were doing file notes or letters after hours? What was that going on?"**

### The "you never told me that" exposure

3. **"Has a client ever come back later and said 'you never told me that' or 'I told you about X' — tell me about a specific time."**
4. **"How do you protect yourself today against a dispute about what was said in a conference?"**

### The recording question — sharpened framing

5. **"When's the last time you had a client conference where you wished you had it on the record? What was happening?"**
6. **"And the opposite — when's the last time you were genuinely grateful nothing was on the record? Walk me through that."**
7. **"If I gave you a tool that handled the consent piece — explicit, timestamped, separately stored, defensible — would the recording sit differently for you? Why / why not?"**
8. **"What about by matter type — does an initial property-settlement consult feel different to a coercive-control matter? Tell me about both."**

### Cross-caseload "what's slipping" (Section 3 probe — verify the pain)

9. **"How do you currently know what's stale across your caseload — what matters where the next move is yours and you haven't made it yet?"**
10. **"Has anything ever fallen through the cracks? An FDR cert? A client follow-up? A court order? Tell me what happened."**

### Current tools

11. **"What practice-management software are you on? When did it last frustrate you?"**
12. **"Have you actively looked at any AI tools for legal practice in the last 6 months? Which ones, and what made you stop or keep going?"**

### The Metis-prepared client probe (5d)

13. **"Walk me through the last time a prospect arrived for a first consult genuinely well-prepared — documents organised, chronology in their head. What did that consult look like compared to the average?"**
14. **"And the opposite — when's the last time the first 30 minutes was just getting their documents into a usable state? What happened?"**
15. **"If a prospect arrived with a document inventory + chronology + draft asset/liability schedule, produced by a tool but reviewed by them — would that be a help, or a threat?"**

### Money / decision

16. **"What do you spend monthly on practice software, and who decides when you add something?"**
17. **"Last time you paid for a new tool — what made it worth it?"**

### Commitment ask (ONLY if genuine heat in the call)

> *"I'm building something in this space and I'd value 30 minutes of your eyes on it next week. Could we book a time?"*

**Real signal:** booked slot, intro to a peer, "can I be a paid beta." **Fake signal:** "send me a link" with no time.

## LISTEN FOR — green vs fake signals

| 🟢 Real signal | 🔴 Fake / noise |
|---|---|
| Unprompted emotion about after-hours notes or a "you never told me that" dispute | "That sounds really useful" |
| Has **already tried/paid** for something to fix it | "AI is the future, good on you" |
| Names a specific dollar figure of wasted time, or a specific recent incident | Future-tense enthusiasm, no past behaviour |
| Open to recording **if X** — gives concrete conditions | Vague "maybe / depends" with no detail |
| Names Law Brief AI / Smokeball Archie / Heidi unprompted with a specific reaction | "I'm sure all these tools are similar" |
| Distinguishes matter type for recording (property = yes, FV = no) | "It depends" with no examples |
| Open to a joint Lawcover conversation | "Once you're SOC 2 maybe" |
| Books the 15-min follow-up AND offers to be quoted (anonymous OK) | Books the slot but won't be named |

---

## 🔪 THE KILL-SIGNAL TO LISTEN FOR

ChatGPT's V2 review: the dangerous outcome isn't *"nobody books."* It's:

> ***"Interesting idea. Call me in a year."***

This means the pain exists but isn't acute enough to change buying behaviour. If you hear this on 4+ of 6 calls, the wedge isn't sharp enough — the message and target are fine; the pain we're attacking isn't where the money is.

Other phrases in the same family:
- *"Once you have customers, come back to me"*
- *"This sounds great for someone bigger / smaller than me"*
- *"Let me think about it"*
- *"What does Lawcover say?"* (without engaging on the joint conversation offer)

## THE THREE RULES — taped to the wall

1. **You are doing research. They are the expert.** If you're talking more than 40% of the time, you're failing.
2. **Past behaviour, not future hypothetical.** "Tell me about the last time…" beats "Would you…" every time.
3. **Compliments mean nothing.** "Sounds great / that's useful / I'd definitely use that" = a fail signal, not a win. **Commitment is the only signal.**
